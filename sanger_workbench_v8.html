Reading a Sanger trace

Chromatogram / trace
The colored peaks showing the sequencing signal for each DNA base.
Base call
The letter a sequencing program assigns to a position. Check the peak when a call looks questionable.
Read
A stretch of DNA sequence produced by one sequencing reaction.
Peak
A rise in one colored signal. A sharp, clearly separated peak usually makes the base easier to read.
Homopolymer
A run of the same base, such as AAAAAA. Individual peaks can merge, making the count uncertain.
Double peak
Two different colored signals at one position. May indicate mixed DNA templates or a heterozygous site.
SNP
Single-nucleotide polymorphism: a difference in one base between DNA versions.
Indel
An insertion or deletion. Mixed templates of different lengths can produce overlapping peaks downstream.
Complement
Swap A↔T and C↔G without changing the order of the bases.
Reverse
Read the sequence in the opposite order without exchanging the base letters.
Reverse complement
Reverse the order and then exchange each base for its complement.
FASTA
A plain-text sequence format with a name line beginning with >.
BLASTN
NCBI search that compares your DNA sequence with nucleotide sequences in a database.
Query cover
How much of your sequence is included in an alignment.
Percent identity
What fraction of aligned positions contain matching bases.
E-value
The expected number of matches at least this strong that could appear by chance in the searched database; smaller is better.
